**Minisymposium**: The next generation: Frontiers in nonlinear waves and pattern formation.

**Abstract**: Pattern formation through Turing bifurcations has been well-studied, and it is well understood. This goes from 'simple' stationary periodic patterns on the line up to stationary localized structures. A lot of this has been discovered through the study of the Ginzburg-Landau equation. Nevertheless, although the complex counterpart of this equation has been studied significantly, there is still a gap to bridge regarding the connection between what is understood for stationary Turing and spatiotemporal Turing-wave instabilities. Specifically, in my talk, I will start by giving general ideas of the study of localized stationary patterns that give rise to the well-known homoclinic snaking, and then I will explain how those ideas could be translated to the spatiotemporal world of the Turing-wave instabilities. Specifically, I will show an example of localized spatiotemporal patterns found through the application of the theory that I will show for Turing.
